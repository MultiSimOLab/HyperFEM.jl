Tensor algebra

HyperFEM works with tensors of order 1 to 4, stored with full indices (Gridap ≥ 0.20). This page states the conventions and lists the changes of the tensor algebra revision, for the users of the former names.

Storage

OrderTypeComponents
1VectorValue{D}v[i]
2TensorValue{D,D}A[i,j]
3ThirdOrderTensorValue{D,D,D}W[i,j,k]
4FourthOrderTensorValue{D}H[i,j,k,l]

FourthOrderTensorValue{D} is an alias of Gridap's HighOrderTensorValue{Tuple{D,D,D,D}}. The components are stored in column-major order (first index fastest).

For linear algebra (inverse, eigenvalues, linear systems), a fourth-order tensor is a D²×D² matrix, the linear map on second-order tensors, with rows (i,j) and columns (k,l). Both hold the same components in the same order. flat_tensor, flat_smatrix and flat_matrix give the matrix (a third-order tensor gives the D×D² matrix, rows i and columns (j,k)), and the constructor applied to the components goes back:

M = flat_tensor(H)                     # TensorValue{D²,D²}; flat_smatrix(H), flat_matrix(H)
H = FourthOrderTensorValue(Tuple(M))   # from a TensorValue{D²,D²}, SMatrix or Matrix (e.g. a ForwardDiff Hessian)
W = ThirdOrderTensorValue(Tuple(M))    # from a D×D² matrix (e.g. a ForwardDiff Jacobian)
H = FourthOrderTensorValue(1.:81...)   # also from the components

Pass the components as a tuple in the constitutive models: splatting 81 components (H...) is not type-stable and allocates.

Operators

OperatorNameContracted indices
a ⊗ bouter productnone
a ⋅ bsingle contractionthe last index of a with the first index of b
a ⋅² bdouble contractionthe last two indices of a with the first two indices of b
a ⊙ bfull contractionall the indices of two tensors of the same order
a × bcross product (3D)the last two indices of a with the first two indices of b
a'transposeW'[i,j,k] = W[k,i,j], H'[i,j,k,l] = H[k,l,i,j]
a * bsame as a ⋅ bsecond-order tensors only, to be removed

⊗, ⋅, ⋅² and ⊙ between tensors of the same order are Gridap's. ⋅² (\cdot\^2) is the alias of Gridap's double_contraction. ⊙ requires tensors of the same order: between tensors of different orders, use ⋅ or ⋅². HyperFEM adds the transposes, the norm of high-order tensors and the cross products. It also redefines, with the same results, the operations that are slower in Gridap for 81 components: +, - and the scaling of high-order tensors, and A ⊗ B of second-order tensors.

The middle dot · (U+00B7) is the same operator as ⋅ (U+22C5): Julia normalizes it when parsing, so A · B and H ·² G are A ⋅ B and H ⋅² G.

Index notation

The products that the operators cannot write are named in index notation: the subscripts are the indices of the first operand and the superscripts those of the second one. The free indices i, j, k, l are the indices of the result, in this order, and m is summed.

A ⊗ᵢₖʲ v      # R[i,j,k]   = A[i,k] v[j]
A ⊗ᵢₖʲˡ B     # R[i,j,k,l] = A[i,k] B[j,l]
A ⊗ᵢₗʲᵏ B     # R[i,j,k,l] = A[i,l] B[j,k]
A ⊗ᵢₘʲᵐᵏˡ H   # R[i,j,k,l] = Σm A[i,m] H[j,m,k,l]

They are typed with the LaTeX completions of the REPL and editors, e.g. \otimes\_i\_k\^j\^l<tab>. Each one is a line in TensorAlgebra/Operations.jl with the index labels of its operands, and einstein_sum (TensorAlgebra/Kernel.jl) generates its code:

(⊗ᵢₘʲᵐᵏˡ)(A::TensorValue, H::FourthOrderTensorValue) = einstein_sum(Val((:i, :m)), Val((:j, :m, :k, :l)), A, H)

Changes of the revision

Storage with full indices

Third-order tensors are ThirdOrderTensorValue{D,D,D} instead of TensorValue{D,D²}, and fourth-order tensors are FourthOrderTensorValue{D} instead of TensorValue{D²,D²}. The components keep their order, so a 9×9 matrix and the corresponding fourth-order tensor hold the same values.

  • Products of fourth-order tensors are written ⋅² instead of * (Gridap's * is an error for them): H ⋅² G, W ⋅² H.
  • H' is the major transpose, as the transpose of the 9×9 matrix was.
  • norm(H) (Frobenius) is defined for high-order tensors, so isapprox works on them.
  • The tangents computed with ForwardDiff are converted with FourthOrderTensorValue(Tuple(M)) and ThirdOrderTensorValue(Tuple(M)) instead of TensorValue(M).
  • regularize_hessian accepts fourth-order tensors.

×ᵢ⁴, IIsym and push_forward_C_to_F keep their names. cof, logreg, ∂log∂J and ∂∂log∂JJ moved to PhysicalModels (Kinematics.jl) and are no longer exported; Tensorize is removed. The cross products (Bonet et al. 2015, Appendix) are generated from their definition, ×ᵢ⁴(A)[i,j,k,l] = εᵢₖₘ εⱼₗₙ Aₘₙ, A × B = ×ᵢ⁴(A) ⋅² B, H × A = H ⋅² ×ᵢ⁴(A), A × H = ×ᵢ⁴(A) ⋅² H and W × A = W ⋅² ×ᵢ⁴(A), with the nonzero terms only: bitwise identical to the same contraction in Gridap, 3-10× faster. A × B and A × H differ from the former hand-written code in the last bit (summation order). The methods cross(::SMatrix, ::SMatrix) and outer(::SVector, ::SVector) are removed (type piracy of LinearAlgebra and StaticArrays).

The products of identities give the same values as the former constants. In a constitutive model, compute them once when the model is built (outside the functions of F), as in LinearElasticity3D.

Fixes

Two former operations computed something different from their name:

  • ⊗₁₂₃⁴ computed Wₖᵢⱼ vₗ instead of Wᵢⱼₖ vₗ. W ⊗ v computes Wᵢⱼₖ vₗ.
  • ⊙₁₂₃₄²⁴ (contraction_IJKL_JL) returned the transpose of its name, Rₖᵢ = Σ Hᵢⱼₖₗ Aⱼₗ.

Neither was used by the constitutive models. All the other operations return results bitwise identical to the former implementation.
