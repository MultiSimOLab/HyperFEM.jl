Time integration

Transient problems are solved by TransientModel on the same incremental loop as the static driver: one attempt per time step with step!, bisection when a solve fails, growth or error-controlled adaptation of the step, internal variables committed when the step is accepted, and the Newton–Raphson solver, line searches and backends of StaticNonlinearModel. This page states the contract the time schemes follow and how a transient problem is written.

The stage contract

A time step is a sequence of stages. Every stage is a (non)linear problem in a stage unknown y (free values). The weak forms do not know the scheme: they receive a context ctx and write the field, its time derivatives and its evaluation points through four functions, each of them affine in y:

field(ctx, u)        # the field of the stage: α₀ y + β₀
∂t(ctx, u)           # first time derivative: α₁ y + β₁
∂tt(ctx, u)          # second time derivative: α₂ y + β₂
at(ctx, u, :np1)     # the field at a point of the step: :n (the history uₙ), :np1 (uₙ₊₁), :stage

α are scalars of the stage (runtime parameters of the forms: no recompilation when Δt changes); β are FE functions over the history vectors of the model, preallocated once and written in place when a step is committed. The Jacobian writes the variations with δfield(ctx, du) = α₀ du, δ∂t(ctx, du) = α₁ du, δ∂tt(ctx, du) and δat(ctx, du, point) on the trial direction. The context also gives the time of the stage ctx.t, the increment ctx.Δt, the history FE functions ctx.u⁻, ctx.u⁻⁻, ctx.v⁻, ctx.a⁻ and the user's ctx.history object. The identity (α = 1, no β: the field of the one-step schemes, the variations) is the unknown itself, so the forms of those schemes are the static forms.

The contract covers implicit one-step and multistep schemes, explicit schemes (the stage unknown is the acceleration and the solve is a mass solve), Runge–Kutta stages (the stage unknown is the stage derivative) and user-defined history objects. One scheme drives every field of a multi-field problem (ctx[i] returns the same context); fields without a time derivative (electric potential, pressure) are evaluated at the same point of the step as the rest and do not enter the error estimate.

Writing a transient problem

General level

The forms take the context. A viscous solid with internal variables, quasi-static in time:

res(ctx) = (u, v)     -> residual(cons_model, k, field(ctx, u), v, dΩ, ctx.t, F∘∇(ctx.u⁻)', A...)
jac(ctx) = (u, du, v) -> jacobian(cons_model, k, field(ctx, u), δfield(ctx, du), v, dΩ, ctx.t, F∘∇(ctx.u⁻)', A...)
m = TransientModel(res, jac, Uu, Vu, D_bc; scheme=QuasiStatic(), xh=uh,
                   physics=cons_model, internal=(xh, xh⁻) -> update_state!(cons_model, A, F∘∇(xh)', F∘∇(xh⁻)'; backend),
                   nls, backend)
solve!(m; stepping=TimeStepping(; Δt=0.05, t_end=15.0, maxbisec=4), post, ProjectDirichlet=true)

Navier–Stokes with stabilisation, where the time derivative also appears inside the SUPG and PSPG terms, and the problem is linear in the unknown once the advecting velocity is taken from the history (Oseen):

function res(ctx)
  ((u, p), (v, q)) -> begin
    w = 2.0 * ctx.u⁻ - 1.0 * ctx.u⁻⁻               # advecting velocity, extrapolated
    Ru = ρ * ∂t(ctx, u) + ρ * (∇(u)' ⋅ w) + ∇(p)
    ∫(ρ * (∂t(ctx, u) ⋅ v))dΩ + … + ∫(τs * ((∇(v)' ⋅ w) ⋅ Ru))dΩ - ∫(τp * (∇(q) ⋅ Ru))dΩ + …
  end
end
m = TransientModel(res, jac, U, V, D_bc; scheme=BDF(2), tangent_update=:step, backend)

Structured level: the mass form

For solids the inertia can be moved out of the forms: the user writes the static forms res(t), jac(t) as for StaticNonlinearModel and gives the bilinear mass form (and a damping form on the velocity); the model assembles the mass matrix once (or once per step with mass_update = :step) and adds the inertia to the residual and the tangent algebraically. This is the fastest path and the one that gives the Armijo line search and the hybrid tangent their incremental energy automatically.

mass = (a, v) -> ∫(ρ * (a ⋅ v))dΩ
m = TransientModel(res, jac, U, V, D_bc; scheme=GeneralizedAlpha(0.9), mass, xh=u0h, vh=v0h,
                   initial_acceleration=:solve, energy, nls, backend)
solve!(m; stepping=AdaptiveStepping(; Δt=1e-3, t_end=2.0, rtol=1e-4), post)

vh is the initial time derivative: the velocity of the second-order schemes, ẋ₀ of the first-order ones. initial_acceleration = :solve takes a₀ from M a₀ = −r(0, u₀) − C v₀ − M_fd g̈₀ (the Dirichlet kinematics at the start from one-sided differences of the prescribed values): with the default :zero the first steps of the second-order schemes see an inconsistent acceleration (their orders and the energy conservation degrade, and the explicit scheme loses one order on a boundary that accelerates from the start); for the first-order schemes it solves M ẋ₀ = −r(0, u₀) − M_fd ġ₀ instead. The adaptive control needs ẋ₀ (or a₀) when the system does not start at rest: the error estimate of the first step is built on the predictor u₀ + Δt ẋ₀, and with ẋ₀ = 0 away from rest every attempt would be rejected. The mass form of a multi-field problem is written on the tuples and is zero on the fields without inertia. The inertia of moving Dirichlet values enters as a load vector.

Tangent update

tangent_update tells the stage solve how the tangent depends on the unknown:

  • :iteration (default): nonlinear problem, the tangent is re-assembled at every Newton iteration;
  • :step: linear in the unknown with a tangent that depends on the history (Oseen): assembled once per attempt and solved once from the zero guess, no Newton iteration and no second residual;
  • :Δt: linear with a tangent that depends on Δt only (heat conduction): assembled and factorised only when Δt or the stage coefficients change (the BDF2 start-up).

Explicit schemes solve with the mass matrix only. The relative criterion of the Newton iteration uses the inertia term at the predictor as reference when it exceeds the initial residual (a good predictor makes ‖R₀‖ tiny, and the relative criterion alone would stall at the round-off floor).

Step control

TimeStepping is the analogue of LoadStepping: fixed Δt, bisection when a solve fails (maxbisec consecutive bisections), growth Δt ← min(grow Δt, Δtmax) after a step converged in at most easy iterations (Δtmax = Δt by default: back to the initial increment after a bisection), exact output times tstops.

AdaptiveStepping controls Δt with the local error estimate of the scheme (Milne's device with extrapolation predictors on the past values for the first-order schemes, Zienkiewicz–Xie for the Newmark family, the embedded pair for SDIRK): the weighted RMS error err over the free dofs with a time derivative, with rtol and atol, must be at most 1 for the step to be accepted; otherwise the step is rejected and retried with the Δt of the controller (PI by default, I on the first step and after a rejection), within [Δtmin, Δtmax] and [fmin, fmax] times the current Δt. A solver failure is not an error estimate: it bisects Δt as in TimeStepping. AdaptiveStepping is an error for schemes without an estimate (QuasiStatic, CentralDifference). A velocity jump at the start (a Dirichlet ramp from rest) gives the Newmark family an estimate that does not decrease with Δt: start such a run with a smooth law (smoothstep) or with TimeStepping.

In both policies a failed or rejected attempt restores the state, the velocities and accelerations, the time, the Dirichlet values and the internal variables of the last accepted step. The loop can be driven by hand: initialize!(m, st) and then step!(m, st, stepping) until finished(st, stepping).

Internal variables and Δt

physics = cons_model makes the driver call update_time_step!(cons_model, Δt) before every attempt with the real Δt (also after a bisection or a rejection). internal = (xh, xh⁻) -> … is called when a step is accepted, with the new state and the state of the previous step, and is where the user updates the internal variables (update_state!, with CellState or KernelState, through the engine when backend = :compiled). Post-processing (post) runs before the internal variables are updated, so it sees the state the residual of the step was evaluated with; inside it get_previous_state(m) is the state of the previous step, and get_velocity/get_acceleration are still those of the previous step (the commit of the scheme comes after).

Energies and output

energy(t) = u -> ∫(Ψ…)dΩ is the stored energy of the model. On the structured level the driver turns it into the incremental functional of the stage whose gradient is the stage residual — the energy of the Armijo line search (InjectivityLS(armijo = true)) and of the hybrid tangent; valid when the residual is the gradient of energy and the mass and damping matrices are symmetric. On the general level the driver cannot derive that functional from the forms: stage_energy(ctx) = y -> … gives it on the stage FE function, or the line searches run without their energy check.

With verbose = 1 one line per time step: step, time, Δt, Newton iterations, residual, the error estimate when adaptive, the kinetic energy K = ½ ∫ρ v⋅v (from the mass form on the velocity of the history, or from the kinetic(vh) functional), the stored energy Π of energy, their sum, the balance W − ΔK − ΔΠ accumulated since the start (W the work of the neumann loads by the trapezoidal rule; the numerical and physical dissipation, the work of loads written inside res and of the Dirichlet reactions are not tracked), convergence and time. With verbose = 2 the header of every step and the Newton iterations. The final summary adds the balance, the accepted, rejected and bisected steps and the range of Δt; get_energies(m) returns the last values.
